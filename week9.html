What is a multiple?
A ratio showing the relationship between two financial metrics (e.g., P/E = Price per share ÷ Earnings per share).
Why use multiples in valuation?
To identify undervalued or overvalued assets, cross-check DCF results, plan exits, and filter investments.
Types of valuation multiples?
Sales (P/S), Earnings (P/E), Book Value (P/B), Enterprise Value (EV/EBITDA), Leverage (Net-debt/EBITDA).
Should we use multiples mechanically?
No. Understand what drives the multiple. Don’t use it as a shortcut for full valuation.
What drives the P/E multiple?
Payout ratio, growth rate (g), and cost of equity (r_e). PE = (Payout × (1+g)) ÷ (r_e – g).
Does a low P/E mean cheap?
Not always. Higher growth or better fundamentals can justify a higher P/E.
What drives the P/B multiple?
Return on equity (ROE), payout ratio, growth, and cost of equity. PBV = (ROE × payout × (1+g)) ÷ (r_e – g).
What drives EV/EBITDA?
Cash flows, tax rate, capex, working capital needs, and cost of capital (k_c).
How to select comparables?
Pick companies with similar risk, sector, growth, and cash flow profiles.
What is the PEG ratio?
P/E divided by expected growth rate. It adjusts P/E for growth differences across firms.
Caveats in using multiples?
Accounting differences, special situations (like restructuring), and oversimplification.
Tip 1: Fix the reference year?
Yes! EV2023 ÷ EBITDA2025 or Price2023 ÷ EPS2025. Keep the base year consistent.
Tip 2: Use forward estimates?
Yes. Use expected EPS, EBITDA, etc., from analysts or DCF forecasts, not historical values.
Steps to compute PE comparison?
Get prices and EPS estimates, compute PE for your firm and peers, compare using averages.
